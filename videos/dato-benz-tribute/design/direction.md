# 以光相伴 — The Light We Carry

The on-screen song is **《兄弟同心》**, dedicated to **Dato Benz**. The film celebrates an admired living person through care, loyalty and the burden that a community learns to share. It should feel like a coherent illustrated cinematic world with Benz inside it, never a slideshow of photo cards.

## The developing world

The first warm light persists through a rain-soaked dark-teal street. It shelters a doorway, reflects across water, reaches a community table and grows into a connected landscape of warm homes. By the last chorus, what began as one light is carried by the whole space. The final portrait belongs to a bright, open environment, with the dedication typeset into genuine negative space.

The light is a visible material cause: rain catches it, wet ground reflects it, a door receives it, glass passes it onward. This gives the camera a route and the edit a carrier. A lit window is intimate; many windows are its consequence. The final chorus gets a new wider dawn world, not the first chorus replayed unchanged.

## Editorial law

Roughly half or more of the film is nonhuman context/detail art. Benz appears at the four named chorus entrances and a few earned verse/final moments. Named shots show one legible, faithful face; intervening shots show the lyric’s meaning rather than another similar portrait pose.

Compose2–3distinct views per major art plate: near object/foreground, middle action, wider consequence. Use rain-glass/door/light carriers to hand off between spaces. Avoid repeated landscape-wide → same landscape-wide cuts, a uniform Ken Burns zoom on every image, a dissolve on every line, decorative HUDs, spectrum bars, unrelated particles or an idle breathing camera.

An original-pixel portrait composite is technically layered, but should not read as a separate photo overlay. Its borders, edge light, environment scale and physical occlusion must be resolved before the full song is rendered.

## Generation prompt contract

Each asset is one separate built-in image call. Use the selected anchor as material/style guidance, and the actual Benz source as the only human identity reference when needed. A style reference is not an appearance source. Environment plates exclude humans, faces, bodies, hands, silhouettes, statues, readable text and logos. Portrait plates contain exactly one Benz, faithful original age/build/clothing and no glamorized body replacement. Avoid adding a plausible event that the photo does not establish.

The final detailed prompts should include the chosen shot’s camera scale, a motivated light source, a foreground occluder, middle/far story detail and a quiet lyric band. Keep each environment distinct while retaining the same weather/material/light journey. Inspect all human portraits side-by-side against the actual source before accepting them into the full edit.
