## Controlled model test

1. **Prepare the originals-only reference board.** Explicitly mark identity, body/wardrobe and environment roles. Do not select an AI-generated face as identity truth. Choose one ordinary lighting setup and actual outfit; avoid changing model, age, clothes, location and camera style at once.
2. **Portrait pair first:** one5-second neutral head/three-quarter turn with Kling O3Pro and one with Seedance2. Use best supported reference controls from the same real material,1080p requested, native audio off. Record any parameter/feature differences; do not pretend the two APIs expose identical settings. Review raw output before ordering body shots.
3. **Only after a convincing portrait:** the same5-second waist-up hand/cup action on both models, then a5-second normal full-body walk on both. If the references do not support a credible body/movement test, use real footage or obtain the missing evidence first. Do not fill the gap with a generic body description.
4. **Review all six complete clips at1× speed, full frame.** Compare with real photos and real motion when available. Inspect suspect moments frame by frame after playback. Check changing face shape, hairline/ears/jaw, normal skin and expressions, torso/head/shoulder proportions, posture and gait, foot contact, hand/object contact, cloth and shadows. Compare the accepted views together across cuts.
5. **User/boss sees a short15–20second pilot** from accepted material before the full song is generated. Supply raw candidates as well as the restrained finished sample. No hard likeness/body/motion failure can be averaged away by attractive lighting or a high similarity score. If neither model passes, change the route toward genuine footage rather than scale the failure.

No new-model superiority is measured yet. Exact-person recognition and naturalness require the people who know him to judge the motion, not just an agent's selected still samples.
