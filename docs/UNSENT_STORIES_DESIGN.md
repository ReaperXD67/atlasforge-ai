# The Unsent Message — design truth

An intimate illustrated evening, not a sales dashboard. Warm cream plaster, terracotta choices and muted teal rooms create a human-scale storybook film. Mira has warm brown skin, dark gathered hair and a teal top; Asha has dark shoulder-length hair and a terracotta top. Their appearance and names stay consistent. Characters are original vector artwork, not copied people or branded mascots. All supplies have generic geometric packaging; Atomy appears only as plain contextual text.

The three skin-tone tokens are illustration-only supporting colors, not extra UI accents. Preserve them consistently across character close-ups and room views.

Use Fraunces 900 for rare display statements and Montserrat 400/700/900 for messages and captions. Ship local font files and licenses. The difference is human narration versus functional phone text. Captions sit on a dark, high-contrast bounded plate, at least 52px portrait / 43px landscape. No more than four words per caption group. Keep important portrait content within x=80..920 and y=200..1650; leave the right-side controls and bottom upload UI clear. Landscape messages must be legible at 360px-wide playback: prefer large, short dialogue over paragraphs.

## Worlds and actions

- Room: window, lamp, sofa, Mira, phone; the eye moves from her face to the screen.
- Phone close-up: authored conversation, one readable message at a time, a draft physically removed, a SEND or call control that changes only when spoken action lands.
- Cupboard: three fixed shelf levels with generic supplies, visible hand-written counts, doors that reveal rather than hide content by accident. No invented Atomy stock/pack-size facts.
- Paper: specific draft lines, a meaningful deletion, one note; never a recycled four-card business grid.
- Calendar: Saturday lunch, not an appointment to pitch; the final state preserves a noncommercial relationship.
- Split-distance room: Mira and Asha in separate rooms with the same conversation as the anchor. No staged customer success.

Every scene has a focal action, room/prop context, an edge-anchored fiction label and a readable supporting detail. First frame already contains a meaningful object. Do not begin with a logo sting. Use restrained drawn structural lines, not film-grain CSS or synthetic camera wobble. Keep the fiction label readable, not a tiny disclaimer.

## Motion and sound

Rhythm: immediate hesitation → context → interruption → recognition → removal → ordinary human reply. Use deterministic object travel, a small camera pose ladder, measured message entry and a final readable hold. Named motion references: discrete-text-sequence, viewport-change, spring-pop-entrance, sine-wave-loop and svg-path-draw. Apply their full contracts before implementation. Characters may be still original illustrations with gentle whole-body reaction/camera motion; don't fake a jointed walking performance with disconnected CSS limbs. Stage the phone, shelf objects and draft slips as the active subjects.

Each scene's meaningful change follows an authored spoken phrase using actual measured word timestamps, not percentages invented from scene length. Match object identity across related cuts. Don't end on black. Use distinct click, cupboard or paper accents sparingly and a low original score with voice-aware carving; the resolution can be quieter than the hook.

## Negative prompt

No copied logos, actual customer screenshots, fake testimonials, before/after efficacy, earnings counters, fake discounts, coercion portrayed as company policy, neon tech cards, default business presenter, endless bullet lists, equal-weight grids, arbitrary particle confetti, disconnected puppet limbs, unreadable phone paragraphs or sales CTA disguised as a friendship ending.
